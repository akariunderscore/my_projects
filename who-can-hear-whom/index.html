who can hear whom

twelve made-up towns, each with an AM transmitter of some range. nobody draws the network, it falls out of the geometry. hover a town, click one, say something

both hear each other   one way only (arrow points at the listener)

the rule, all of it: town A hears town B if A sits inside B's circle. that's it. ranges differ, so plenty of pairs only work in one direction, a big station shouting at a small one that can't shout back. on hover: amber is who hears this town, blue is who it hears, and the count is how far a message could get if every town that heard it repeated it on

messages: five characters a second, one ring per letter. the further out a listener sits in the speaker's circle, the more letters it loses (the · is a letter that didn't make it). once a town has heard the whole thing it repeats it on, as it heard it, so the gaps stack up hop by hop and the colour drifts amber to blue as it goes. a town only keeps the first version that reaches it, which is roughly how rumours work too

the honest line: this is not radio. no propagation physics, no terrain, no skywave (which is the whole reason AM goes far at night, and it's not here), no interference, no antennas. a range is a circle because circles are easy, and letters dropping with distance is a dice roll i made up. it's a toy about graphs that happen to have a map underneath, not a map that happens to have a graph on it
